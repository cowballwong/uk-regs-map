    What this is

    One network of the building regulations system in England: the Acts, the regulations made under them, Schedule 1 of the Building Regulations 2010, every Approved Document in force, HSE's CDM guidance and the 455 British Standards that the Approved Documents cite. Select any dot (click or tap) to see what it cites, what cites it, and the sentence that makes the link.

    How to read the lines

    • Solid line (836): a citation found in the citing document's own words. Select a dot, then press Quote on a row in its panel to read the quoted sentence and where it is (PDF page; an index paragraph or table only where the Approved Document gives one, see below). Types: cites, explains (an Approved Document and the Part it gives guidance on), made under (a regulation and its enabling Act, from the "powers conferred" sentence of its introduction) and amends (where the operative text says the other instrument "is amended", or amends one of its provisions with "In ... of the other instrument ... substitute / insert / omit"; never from footnotes or explanatory notes, and never from words that an amendment inserts into another instrument; the amending provisions of the Building Safety Act 2022 and the Fire Safety Act 2021 are read from their revised text). Two instruments can be joined by two lines, for example made under and amends.
    • Grey tree line: the Building Regulations 2010 and its Schedule 1 Parts A to T and regulation 7 (19 provisions). Each Approved Document has a solid line to its Part.
    • Dashed line (17): a link suggested by AI where documents are used together but neither cites the other. Each has a written reason. It is not a citation.
    • Where to look: every quoted line gives the PDF page on which the quote is printed. "AD index" gives the paragraphs that the Approved Document's own printed index lists for that standard (AD B Volumes 1 and 2, AD K and AD M Volume 1). "In Table 6.2" is shown only where that table's caption is printed on the same page as the quote. No other paragraph numbers are shown: worked out from the PDF text, they would be a guess, not the Approved Document's own reference.
    • Volumes: where an Approved Document names another one that has two volumes (AD B, F, L, M and R), the line goes to the volume it names. Where it names no volume, a volume of a two-volume Approved Document is linked to the volume with the same number (AD L Volume 1 to AD M Volume 1), and any other Approved Document is linked to both volumes. The panel says which rule made the line: "volume not named; linked to the volume with the same number as the citing AD" or "volume not named, so both volumes are linked". No caption means the quoted text names the volume.
    • In force: for an Approved Document this means the edition approved under section 6 of the Building Act 1984 that has taken effect. This site says "take effect" for Approved Documents (the words of the section 6 notices of approval) and "come into force" for Acts and regulations.
    • Topic areas (coloured regions in the domain and subject views) follow the Parts of Schedule 1 and the main regimes. Topics are never drawn as lines.

    British Standards

    • Each standard shows only its number, title and edition as printed in the Approved Document that cites it (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence). Where two Approved Documents cite different editions, both are shown.
    • The standards themselves are BSI publications. Their text is not reproduced, and BSI product pages are not linked: BSI's website terms permit linking to its home page, and they do not allow commercial collection of its product listings.
    • Edition status for 85 standards was checked on 9 November 2026 against public pages not run by BSI: mainly the NBS Publication Index, plus trade bodies, standards retailers, ISO and other national standards bodies. NBS and the retailers republish BSI catalogue data, so they are not independent of BSI's own records. Each flag names its source, and every source was opened again to confirm it. A British Standard numbered BS EN or BS EN ISO is the UK adoption of a European standard, published with the same text by every national standards body (for example SS-EN in Sweden, NEN-EN in the Netherlands, DIN EN in Germany); where a source names another national adoption of the same EN edition, that is read as the same edition of the BS EN. Confirm with BSI before relying on an edition.
    • One rule for every flag: a cited edition shows "newer edition exists" when a later edition, or a later +A amendment of the same edition, has been published. Corrigenda do not count. A successor with a different number (a series split or a replacement series) makes the cited edition "withdrawn" where a source shows it withdrawn, otherwise "newer edition exists"; where both apply, "withdrawn" is shown. A cited edition that is still the newest edition of its own number stays "cited edition current" even where a source calls it "superseded but remains current" because a European standard with another number replaces it in principle (BS 476-20, -21 and -22). The status is worked out for each Approved Document that cites the standard, and for each edition that document prints anywhere (its standards list, its text or its index; some print two). Where these citations have different results, the dot says "depends on citing AD" and the panel gives one row per Approved Document and edition, with the edition and title that document prints. Result: newer edition exists 40, cited edition withdrawn 12, cited edition current 25, depends on citing AD 7, inconclusive 1 (shown as not checked). The other standards say "not checked": no status is guessed from age.
    • An Approved Document cites a specific edition. Using a newer edition is a designer's judgement to agree with building control.

    Limits

    • Links are at document level; the Approved Document paragraphs and the regulations inside each instrument are not separate dots yet.
    • The 2026 editions of AD L (both volumes) and AD F Volume 1 (approved by Circular 01/2026 and revised by Circular 04/2026; they take effect on 24 Mar 2027, or 24 Sep 2027 for higher-risk building work) are not shown. AD B is shown as published in 2026, which already prints the changes that take effect on 2 Sep 2029.
    • Quotes come from machine-extracted PDF text and were spot-checked against the PDFs. Line breaks, hyphens and two-column layouts can still garble a word.
    • Lines between pieces of legislation come from the text as made (introduction and operative provisions); Schedule 1, the Building Safety Act 2022 and the Fire Safety Act 2021 come from the revised text on legislation.gov.uk on the check date, which may not yet include every amendment.
    • Earlier amending regulations are not separate dots, because their changes are already in the revised Building Regulations 2010 and its Schedule 1 shown here: SI 2021/1391 (the 2021 Parts L, F and O, in force 15 Jun 2022; its regulation 17 is the transitional provision for them (paragraph (1) as substituted by SI 2023/520); SI 2026/335 regulation 10 omits regulation 17(1)(a) from 24 Mar 2027, subject to savings), SI 2021/1392 (Part S), SI 2022/603 (regulation 7(1A) and the hotels, hostels and boarding houses added to regulation 7(2), 1 Dec 2022), SI 2022/984 (Part R, RA1 and RA2, 26 Dec 2022), SI 2023/520 (corrections to SI 2021/1391 and SI 2022/603) and SI 2024/645 (Part T, 1 Oct 2024). Their commencement and transitional rules are in each instrument on legislation.gov.uk. The amending regulations that are dots are those that bring in the 2023 building safety regime or change it later (for example SI 2023/911, SI 2025/1017 and SI 2026/1034), and SI 2026/335, which is not yet in force. One later amending instrument is not a dot: SI 2026/1094, mainly about local authority charges (see Not in the map).
    • Not in the map: the Building Safety Act commencement regulations (for example SI 2023/993); the Building (Local Authority Charges and Amendments Relating to Charging) (England) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/1094, in force 6 Nov 2026; it replaces the Building (Local Authority Charges) Regulations 2010 in England. Its regulation 11 is not about charges: it revokes the Building (Amendment) Regulations 2013 in England and substitutes regulation 11(3) of the Building Regulations 2010, which then bars relaxation under section 8 of the Building Act 1984 only for regulation 23(1)(a) and paragraph R1 of Schedule 1; regulation 11 comes into force on 24 Mar 2027, or 24 Sep 2027 in relation to higher-risk buildings); the Responsible Actors Scheme and its building control prohibition (SI 2023/753); the Building Safety Act's remediation and leaseholder protection provisions; the planning Gateway 1 fire statement rules; directions under section 11 of the Building Act 1984 that dispense with procedural requirements (for example Circulars 02/2026 and 03/2026, for telecommunications work); the construction products regime; the Manual to the Building Regulations; and other guidance that the Approved Documents point to.
