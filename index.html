    UK Building Regulations Map

    How England's building regulations, Approved Documents and the British Standards they cite link together. Every citation line is quoted from its source, including the orange dashed lines a search preview draws to documents outside it. The white dashed lines are AI suggestions, labelled as such; grey lines show how the Regulations are laid out.

    Which edition do the Approved Documents cite?

    Paste a specification clause, a schedule or a list of standard numbers. The map picks out every standard number in it (BS, BS EN, BS EN ISO, BS ISO, BS IEC, BS EN IEC, PD, PAS, DD or CP; a bare EN, ISO or IEC number is matched only to its UK adoption with the same number) and shows which Approved Documents cite it, the edition they print, and what our edition check found.

    Not a compliance check

    It reports only what the Approved Documents print and what our edition check found on 9 November 2026. "Status not checked" means we have not checked that standard, not that it is current. Statuses come only from the public sources listed, none run by BSI. It cannot tell which edition applies to your project: that depends on the Approved Document in force for your application and its transitional provisions. Many Approved Documents say a newer version of a listed standard may be used as guidance if it still addresses the requirements. Everything runs in your browser: nothing you paste is sent anywhere.

              Disclaimer

              This is a personal hobby project by Anzon Wong (AW Design). It is not an official website and does not represent, and is not endorsed by, the Ministry of Housing, Communities and Local Government, the Building Safety Regulator, the Health and Safety Executive, The National Archives or BSI. It is a navigation aid only and is not legal or professional advice. Always check the official text before relying on anything shown here. It covers England only: Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own rules. The edition that applies to a project can depend on transitional provisions (when the notice or application was made and when work started); the edition shown is the one in force for new applications on the data date, 9 November 2026, including any text it already prints for later dates.

              What this is

              One network of the building regulations system in England: the Acts, the regulations made under them, Schedule 1 of the Building Regulations 2010, every Approved Document in force, HSE's CDM guidance and the 455 British Standards that the Approved Documents cite. Select any dot (click or tap) to see what it cites, what cites it, and the sentence that makes the link.

              How to read the lines

              • Solid line (836): a citation found in the citing document's own words. Select a dot, then press Quote on a row in its panel to read the quoted sentence and where it is (PDF page; an index paragraph or table only where the Approved Document gives one, see below). Types: cites, explains (an Approved Document and the Part it gives guidance on), made under (a regulation and its enabling Act, from the "powers conferred" sentence of its introduction) and amends (where the operative text says the other instrument "is amended", or amends one of its provisions with "In ... of the other instrument ... substitute / insert / omit"; never from footnotes or explanatory notes, and never from words that an amendment inserts into another instrument; the amending provisions of the Building Safety Act 2022 and the Fire Safety Act 2021 are read from their revised text). Two instruments can be joined by two lines, for example made under and amends.
              • Grey tree line: the Building Regulations 2010 and its Schedule 1 Parts A to T and regulation 7 (19 provisions). Each Approved Document has a solid line to its Part.
              • Dashed line (17): a link suggested by AI where documents are used together but neither cites the other. Each has a written reason. It is not a citation.
              • Where to look: every quoted line gives the PDF page on which the quote is printed. "AD index" gives the paragraphs that the Approved Document's own printed index lists for that standard (AD B Volumes 1 and 2, AD K and AD M Volume 1). "In Table 6.2" is shown only where that table's caption is printed on the same page as the quote. No other paragraph numbers are shown: worked out from the PDF text, they would be a guess, not the Approved Document's own reference.
              • Volumes: where an Approved Document names another one that has two volumes (AD B, F, L, M and R), the line goes to the volume it names. Where it names no volume, a volume of a two-volume Approved Document is linked to the volume with the same number (AD L Volume 1 to AD M Volume 1), and any other Approved Document is linked to both volumes. The panel says which rule made the line: "volume not named; linked to the volume with the same number as the citing AD" or "volume not named, so both volumes are linked". No caption means the quoted text names the volume.
              • In force: for an Approved Document this means the edition approved under section 6 of the Building Act 1984 that has taken effect. This site says "take effect" for Approved Documents (the words of the section 6 notices of approval) and "come into force" for Acts and regulations.
              • Topic areas (coloured regions in the domain and subject views) follow the Parts of Schedule 1 and the main regimes. Topics are never drawn as lines.

              British Standards

              • Each standard shows only its number, title and edition as printed in the Approved Document that cites it (Crown copyright, Open Government Licence). Where two Approved Documents cite different editions, both are shown.
              • The standards themselves are BSI publications. Their text is not reproduced, and BSI product pages are not linked: BSI's website terms permit linking to its home page, and they do not allow commercial collection of its product listings.
              • Edition status for 85 standards was checked on 9 November 2026 against public pages not run by BSI: mainly the NBS Publication Index, plus trade bodies, standards retailers, ISO and other national standards bodies. NBS and the retailers republish BSI catalogue data, so they are not independent of BSI's own records. Each flag names its source, and every source was opened again to confirm it. A British Standard numbered BS EN or BS EN ISO is the UK adoption of a European standard, published with the same text by every national standards body (for example SS-EN in Sweden, NEN-EN in the Netherlands, DIN EN in Germany); where a source names another national adoption of the same EN edition, that is read as the same edition of the BS EN. Confirm with BSI before relying on an edition.
              • One rule for every flag: a cited edition shows "newer edition exists" when a later edition, or a later +A amendment of the same edition, has been published. Corrigenda do not count. A successor with a different number (a series split or a replacement series) makes the cited edition "withdrawn" where a source shows it withdrawn, otherwise "newer edition exists"; where both apply, "withdrawn" is shown. A cited edition that is still the newest edition of its own number stays "cited edition current" even where a source calls it "superseded but remains current" because a European standard with another number replaces it in principle (BS 476-20, -21 and -22). The status is worked out for each Approved Document that cites the standard, and for each edition that document prints anywhere (its standards list, its text or its index; some print two). Where these citations have different results, the dot says "depends on citing AD" and the panel gives one row per Approved Document and edition, with the edition and title that document prints. Result: newer edition exists 40, cited edition withdrawn 12, cited edition current 25, depends on citing AD 7, inconclusive 1 (shown as not checked). The other standards say "not checked": no status is guessed from age.
              • An Approved Document cites a specific edition. Using a newer edition is a designer's judgement to agree with building control.

              Edition check and spec check

              • Edition check: the button on the start panel (and "Edition check 52" at the top right) shows the 52 standards that an Approved Document cites in an edition for which a public source not run by BSI shows a newer edition or amendment (40, of which 3 are a later amendment to the same edition) or a withdrawal (12). Select one to see the Approved Document's own sentence, the PDF page, the edition it prints, and the source we checked. The 7 standards whose status depends on the citing Approved Document are listed and shown separately and are not in the count. Many Approved Documents say the edition they list is the relevant one and that a newer version may be used as guidance if it still addresses the requirements; the card quotes that sentence where the citing Approved Document has it. So the check is a prompt to look, not a ban, and it cannot say which edition applies to a project. 84 of the 455 standards have a confirmed status; the other 371 are unknown, not current.
              • Check my spec: paste text and the page finds standard numbers in it (BS, BS EN, BS EN ISO, BS ISO, BS IEC, BS EN IEC, PD, PAS, DD and CP; with or without spaces or colons, with years and amendments such as 2018+A2:2022, and parts written "-1" or "Part 1"). A standard is matched only on its prefix, number and part together: BS 845-1 and BS EN 845-1 are different standards. A bare EN, ISO or IEC number is matched only to its UK adoption with the same number, and a part that is not on the map is never matched to the whole standard. For each one it reports whether the map has it, which Approved Documents cite it, every edition each prints (with the PDF page), whether your edition or amendment differs from them, and the status from our check with its source. You can download the table as a CSV file. It runs in your browser and sends nothing anywhere. It is not a compliance check: it does not say which edition applies to your project, and a status is only as verified on the date shown.
              • Hidden until you zoom: the 371 standards whose status has not been checked are hidden in the overview so the checked ones stand out. They all appear when you zoom in yourself (wheel, pinch or +). A search shows the one you searched for, selecting a document shows only the unchecked standards it cites, and "Show unchecked standards" in the legend shows them all.

              Full text of the Regulations and the Approved Documents

              • What is here: the full text of the Building Regulations 2010 (England text, the revised version valid from 6 November 2026 on legislation.gov.uk: 1,736 provisions, the 65 Schedule 1 requirements that apply in England, each with its limits on application) and of the 24 Approved Documents in force (1,625 PDF pages from GOV.UK), with their clause numbers, headings, paragraphs, lists, notes and tables. Diagrams, and tables that are not ruled in the PDF, are given as their caption with a link to that PDF page. Every block shows its PDF page. Open it from an Approved Document, a Schedule 1 Part, regulation 7 or SI 2010/2214 ("Full text" in the panel), or with "Read and search the full text".
              • England text: legislation.gov.uk prints the England and Wales wording of the Regulations side by side. Using its own markings (each change says whether it was made for England or for Wales), the reader leaves out 41 provisions that apply only in Wales (each counted once with everything under it, for example Part 2B with the Welsh dutyholder regulations 11R to 11Z8, and requirement G2A), the Wales versions of 30 provisions whose England version is shown, and 19 Wales-only lines inside provisions that apply in England (for example the Welsh definitions of "higher-risk building" and "principal designer" in regulation 2(1)), and shows the England wording where the two differ inside one line. Where legislation.gov.uk gives only the Welsh wording of a few words, the line is shown as printed and says so.
              • From a requirement to its guidance: each Schedule 1 requirement links to the section of the Approved Document that gives guidance on it, and that section links back to the requirement. A quote on a citation line opens the Approved Document at the paragraph it comes from, with the quote highlighted.
              • AD B and its 2029 amendments: the GOV.UK PDFs of AD B Vol 1 and Vol 2 also print the text that takes effect on 2 September 2029. Its pages are marked "2029 text, in force from 2 September 2029" in the reader and in search results, and the pages it will replace are marked "In force until 1 September 2029". A quote is opened in the 2029 text only when it is not in the text in force, and the button says so.
              • Search: words (hyphenated words too, such as fire-resisting or U-value) or a clause number (2.5, B1, regulation 7, or B1 2.5 for paragraph 2.5 of the guidance on B1) across the Regulations and all 24 Approved Documents, or one of them. A result opens the text at that clause and flies the map to its document. The search index and each document load only when you search or open them.
              • How it was made and checked: the Approved Document text was extracted from the GOV.UK PDFs and checked against a second, independent extraction: 99.808% of its 2,669,652 characters match. It is then cleaned in stated steps, each checked: control characters left by the PDFs (their tab marks) are removed, checked against a third tool's reading; 1,185 words whose "fi", "fl" or "ff" the PDFs' text layer loses ("fre" for "fire") are repaired where the PDF's own glyphs show the ligature; running section titles at the top of continuation pages are shown in the page bar, not as paragraphs. Three paragraph numbers the extraction missed are restored and marked in the text: AD B Vol 1 B16 (printed twice) and AD M Vol 2 5.19 are split at the printed number, and AD G A27, which both tools read after A28, is marked. The Regulations are legislation.gov.uk's XML; all 3,420 lines were checked against its own rendition. Changes that legislation.gov.uk lists as not yet applied (for example those of SI 2026/335 from 24 March 2027) are flagged on the provision they affect and are not applied.
              • Licence: Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The Approved Documents are Crown copyright, published on GOV.UK; the Building Regulations 2010 come from legislation.gov.uk (The National Archives). Each reader names its source and edition. No British Standard text is reproduced: only the Approved Documents' own references to standards.
              • Limits: the text is this site's reading of the PDFs, not an official version: some words that lose an "fi" or "fl" where the PDF gives no glyph evidence are shown as stored (search finds them either way, and each reader that has any says so), a symbol the PDF does not store as text is marked, a merged table cell may be split across rows, and paragraph breaks across columns or pages can fall in the wrong place. Read the PDF or legislation.gov.uk before relying on it. It is not legal advice.

              Limits

              • Links are at document level; the Approved Document paragraphs and the regulations inside each instrument are not separate dots yet.
              • The 2026 editions of AD L (both volumes) and AD F Volume 1 (approved by Circular 01/2026 and revised by Circular 04/2026; they take effect on 24 Mar 2027, or 24 Sep 2027 for higher-risk building work) are not shown. AD B is shown as published in 2026, which already prints the changes that take effect on 2 Sep 2029.
              • Quotes come from machine-extracted PDF text and were spot-checked against the PDFs. Line breaks, hyphens and two-column layouts can still garble a word.
              • Lines between pieces of legislation come from the text as made (introduction and operative provisions); Schedule 1, the Building Safety Act 2022 and the Fire Safety Act 2021 come from the revised text on legislation.gov.uk on the check date, which may not yet include every amendment.
              • Earlier amending regulations are not separate dots, because their changes are already in the revised Building Regulations 2010 and its Schedule 1 shown here: SI 2021/1391 (the 2021 Parts L, F and O, in force 15 Jun 2022; its regulation 17 is the transitional provision for them (paragraph (1) as substituted by SI 2023/520); SI 2026/335 regulation 10 omits regulation 17(1)(a) from 24 Mar 2027, subject to savings), SI 2021/1392 (Part S), SI 2022/603 (regulation 7(1A) and the hotels, hostels and boarding houses added to regulation 7(2), 1 Dec 2022), SI 2022/984 (Part R, RA1 and RA2, 26 Dec 2022), SI 2023/520 (corrections to SI 2021/1391 and SI 2022/603) and SI 2024/645 (Part T, 1 Oct 2024). Their commencement and transitional rules are in each instrument on legislation.gov.uk. The amending regulations that are dots are those that bring in the 2023 building safety regime or change it later (for example SI 2023/911, SI 2025/1017 and SI 2026/1034), and SI 2026/335, which is not yet in force. One later amending instrument is not a dot: SI 2026/1094, mainly about local authority charges (see Not in the map).
              • Not in the map: the Building Safety Act commencement regulations (for example SI 2023/993); the Building (Local Authority Charges and Amendments Relating to Charging) (England) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/1094, in force 6 Nov 2026; it replaces the Building (Local Authority Charges) Regulations 2010 in England. Its regulation 11 is not about charges: it revokes the Building (Amendment) Regulations 2013 in England and substitutes regulation 11(3) of the Building Regulations 2010, which then bars relaxation under section 8 of the Building Act 1984 only for regulation 23(1)(a) and paragraph R1 of Schedule 1; regulation 11 comes into force on 24 Mar 2027, or 24 Sep 2027 in relation to higher-risk buildings); the Responsible Actors Scheme and its building control prohibition (SI 2023/753); the Building Safety Act's remediation and leaseholder protection provisions; the planning Gateway 1 fire statement rules; directions under section 11 of the Building Act 1984 that dispense with procedural requirements (for example Circulars 02/2026 and 03/2026, for telecommunications work); the construction products regime; the Manual to the Building Regulations; and other guidance that the Approved Documents point to.
